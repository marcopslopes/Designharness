# Northline Voice & Tone (example pack)

How Northline sounds to store staff and their trade customers. Use when writing UI copy, notifications, onboarding screens or any product communication.

**Prevents: Content drift** — a screen that's visually correct but sounds like generic SaaS copy.

**Confidence: EXAMPLE — Northline's voice is invented for this harness. Replace with your own style guide, keeping the Do/Don't table shape.**

---

## Who we're talking to

**Store staff (Northline Counter)** — people on a shop floor who often read the app with one hand free, between customers. They want the app to take a job off their plate, not to teach them something.

**Trade customers (Northline Trade)** — people ordering from their regular store. They want to check stock, order or pay in a few taps and get back to the job.

---

## Personality

- **Calm** — the app never adds stress to a busy counter
- **Specific** — names the item, the quantity, the amount
- **On your side** — talks like a good colleague on the shop floor, not a vendor

**Not** chirpy, salesy, technical or precious.

---

## Voice principles

### Say what changed, then what to do
> ✓ "The paint delivery moved to 3:30. Aisle 4 is still clear."
> ✗ "A delivery has been rescheduled. Please review your orders."

### Lead with the job, not the feature
> ✓ "Ask for a deposit on special orders."
> ✗ "Turn on Northline Holds."

### Numbers beat adjectives
Don't say "Lots of your regulars are overdue" — say "6 trade accounts are past their usual reorder date." Name the count, the accounts, and one next step.

### Say how long it takes
"Takes about 2 minutes" belongs on any setup step longer than one screen.

### Quiet confirmations
Confirm routine actions in a few words ("Saved.", "Order sent to Ana."). Save warmth for real milestones like a first payout.

## Writing rules

| Rule | Do | Don't |
|------|----|-------|
| **Sentence case** for UI text | "Send a reminder" | "Send A Reminder" |
| **No exclamation marks** in routine copy | "Your first payout is on its way." | "Payout sent!" |
| **Full stops on body text** | Complete sentences end with a period | Fragment body copy |
| **Verb-first buttons**, 1–3 words | "Add deposit" · "Reorder paint" | "Click here to add a deposit" |
| **Name the item** in notifications | "M8 bolts back in stock" | "An item is back in stock" |
| **Product names in title case** | "Northline Holds" | "northline holds" |
| **Skip "manage"** on buttons | Use the actual action ("Edit hours") | "Manage hours" |
| **No hype** | State what happened and what's next | "Supercharge your sales!" |

## Vocabulary (example)

| Use | Avoid | Notes |
|-----|-------|-------|
| **Trade customers** | "Users", "consumers" | Store-side copy |
| **Order** | "Transaction" | Store side; customer side prefers "order" too |
| **Store** | "Merchant", "vendor" | "Store" in docs, "you" in UI |
| **Deposit** | "Prepayment", "down payment" | — |
| **Reorder** | "Re-engage", "win back" | Verb for the next order of a regular line |
| **Refer** | "Invite", "share" | Referral program verb; flag to localization early |

## Tone by surface

| Surface | Tone notes |
|---------|-----------|
| Onboarding | Encouraging, short, one task per screen. |
| Notifications | Specific and calm — what, when, what next. |
| Empty states | Explain what will appear here and the one action that fills it. |
| Error messages | Plain cause, no blame, a way forward. |
| Marketing / referral copy | Upbeat but concrete — real rewards, real numbers. |
| Settings | Neutral and descriptive. |

---

## Negative examples — what off-brand sounds like

- "Supercharge your sales!" — hype with no information
- "You're crushing it!" — praise for nothing in particular
- "Whoops! Our bad 🙈" — emoji and cutesy tone
- "Leverage Bulletins to drive retention" — jargon
- "An item ran out" — missing the item's name
- "Manage your deposits" — vague button

---

## Never do this

- Never use exclamation marks to make a routine action sound exciting.
- Never use emoji in product copy.
- Never call a store's trade customers "users".
- Never send a notification without a named item or a number, plus one next action.
- Never use "manage" as a button verb.
- Never write copy that could belong to any other app.
- Never treat this file as final — check with your content team before locking a rule.
